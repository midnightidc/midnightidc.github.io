It's kind of devolved, how much I want to be known. I think it's more of a fear I have, to prove myself so I will be remembered for the work I did. Maybe it's because ever since i've gone to vietnam, majority of my acomplishments have been centered around the fact that I was born an englisg speaker (not that that changes anything in my learning and comprehension).
But because of these circumstances, I often end up wanting to prove myself and do better, but it's also one of my biggest fears to give it my all and fail; because then, if I fail, my all just isnt enough to succeed, and I **hate** the idea of that. To the point where I used to do all these tests, and not even study for them.

Keyword *used to*. To be fair, I am still yet to figure out if I am giving it my all, since I have never really gave my all, but nowadays I am trying harder than ever, since, well... My mentality has changed. I want to be someone great, who will inspire other great people.

My main inspiration right now is the exhilaration (idk if i spelt it right), when I gave it my all and got in, as well as all the encouragement I recieved from my loved ones. Once I got into the neuroscience team, and saw all these people, happy for what **I** had done, all my fears flaked off me like sand in wind, and the only thing I had left was the urge to try harder. Last weekend, I had two celebratory meals, gift money, so many people cheering for me, and my passion.
Right where I left it as a child, practically untouched, but ready to re-ignite and burn brighter.

I really am glad I gave it my all, and I'm even gladder I got in.
